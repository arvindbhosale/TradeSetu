Markets

Each card is an index of its stocks, starting at 100 a year ago, equal-weighted unless you change the weights. Trend compares the index with its 20 and 50-day averages. The sectors are a curated starting set of liquid NSE stocks, not an exchange classification: open a card to add or remove stocks and change weights.

Daily candles of the sector index (built from its stocks; high and low approximate)

Stocks in this sector

Weight is each stock's share of the index. Enter any numbers; they are scaled to 100%. A weight of 0 keeps the stock listed but out of the index. Click a stock to open its chart.

Index levels and candles come from Kite when it is logged in (the official index values), otherwise from Yahoo where it carries the index. Constituents are NSE's published lists, refreshed daily.

New algo

    
An algo runs one strategy on one stock by itself. The easiest way to make one is to backtest first, then press Create algo under the result. Day candles: delivery (CNC). It reads the day's candle after the close and sends its order when your trade window opens next session; the target and stoploss are placed as a GTT. Intraday candles: intraday (MIS). It trades inside your trade window, watches the target and stoploss on the live price, and sells everything at the sell-everything (square-off) time.

Algos

    

  

  

    Before you run one live. Kite signs you out every morning: sign in to Kite before your trade window opens, or a live algo cannot trade that day and says so. Orders are limit orders near the last price; one that does not fill is cancelled and the algo stays as it was. Intraday algos need live candles: Yahoo's are delayed, so a live intraday algo uses Kite's historical-data add-on when your API key has it and refuses to trade on stale candles. Your risk limits and the kill switch apply to every algo order. The desk checks about every 20 seconds; it is not a high-frequency engine.
  

  

  

    

      
New F&O algo

      
Runs an option strategy or an index future by itself, on Kite's live prices. The easiest way to make one is to backtest first (Backtest → Option strategy), then press Create algo under the result. Paper fills in the paper book at the last price; Live sends real orders. Both take the same path: every leg is a limit order through your risk limits. Bought legs (hedges) are bought first and sold legs are sold only after every hedge has filled; on exit the sold legs are bought back first.

F&O algos

      

    

    

      Before you run one live. Option prices come only from Kite, so a paper F&O algo needs Kite too. MCX metals run in the MCX session (09:00 to 23:30, or 23:55 while the US is on winter time) with entry and exit times up to 23:25, take funds from Kite's commodity segment, and close every contract once it is within the delivery cut-off in Settings (8 days by default): gold and silver contracts are physically delivered and Zerodha does not take retail positions into delivery. Since 1 April 2026 Kite accepts API orders only from a static IP registered on your Kite Connect app (IP Whitelist), at most 10 a second: run kite-mcp-server on a machine with that IP. A live algo needs a maximum loss; when the position's loss with charges reaches it every leg is closed and the algo waits for the next day. Structures that sell options with no bought option to cover them run live only when Settings allows naked F&O shorts. Margin is the desk's estimate (Kite's MCP server has no margin calculator); Zerodha decides at order time, and a refused leg unwinds the entry. Every leg is closed at the exit time on its expiry day. Exit closes everything now, even with the kill switch on.

Breakout scanner

    
Scans a list of stocks on daily candles for a price-and-volume breakout that is still holding near its high, then waits for a pullback: a buy GTT at the breakout candle's low, and a stoploss once that buy triggers. Each setup is acted on once, however often you scan.

Index futures backtest

      
Buys or sells index futures in lots when a strategy says so: buy only, sell only, or both (always in the market). Day candles are Kite's continuous futures series; every monthly roll is charged and the basis jump is taken out. Intraday runs use the live contract's candles where they reach back far enough, otherwise the index as a proxy. Futures are leveraged: a 1% move is about 8% of the margin.

      

        

          
          
          
          
          
        

        

        

        

          
          
          
          
          
        

        

          
          
          
          
        

      

      

    

  

  

    

      
Options buying backtest (simulated)

      
Buys index options when a strategy says so: calls when it says buy, puts when it says sell, or both. Kite keeps no candles for expired contracts, so premiums are modelled with Black-Scholes from the index's real candles and India VIX. Real premiums differ, most around events and on expiry day.

Trades

        

      

      

        How to read this. A buy or sell decided on a candle's close happens at the next candle's open, with slippage and Zerodha charges on every order. Day candles trade as delivery (CNC). Intraday candles trade as intraday (MIS): buys only between your start and stop times, everything sold at the sell-everything time. Stocks are bought, never sold short, in whole shares. It ignores liquidity, circuit-limit days, tax on gains and delisted stocks, so real results would be lower. Past returns do not predict future ones.

Runs the full agent pipeline on each symbol and date, then scores every rating against the Nifty over the next 5 trading days. Each cell is a full analysis: it is slow and costs LLM credits. The model may also know what happened after a past date, so treat results as indicative.

MCX gold & silver futures

      

    

    

    

    
Priced in rupees on MCX: the international gold or silver price times the rupee rate, plus import duty. Kite takes MCX quantity in lots. "₹ per lot" is the rupee value of one lot (GOLD is quoted per 10 g in 1 kg lots, so a ₹1 move is ₹100 a lot); margin is the desk's estimate at the MCX margin % in Settings, and Zerodha decides at order time. Contracts inside the delivery cut-off are marked: the desk opens nothing in them.

  

  

    

      

    

    

    

    

      
      
      
      
      
    

    

    

  

  

    
MCX option chain

      
        
        
        
      

    

    

    

    
MCX options are written on a futures contract, not on spot: the chain is centred on that future, and IV and delta are Black-76. An in-the-money option left to expiry turns into that future, which is then physically delivered, so the delivery cut-off applies to options too. Click a price to trade it.

  

  

    
MCX positions 

    

  

  
How MCX trading works here. Same Kite account, commodity segment (activate it in Zerodha Console first). Every order is a LIMIT order through your risk limits: kill switch, MCX trade window, order value (a future is weighed by its margin), orders per day. Manual orders buy, and sell what you hold; a short position is opened only by an armed F&O algo (Algos → Options & Futures, pick a metal). Physical delivery: every gold and silver contract here is compulsorily delivered. Zerodha squares off positions before the delivery period and publishes the date for each expiry ("MCX precious metals expiry" bulletin, usually 3 to 7 days before expiry). The desk takes no new position within the cut-off in Settings, and closes paper positions there, the way Zerodha would close a real one. Session: 09:00 to 23:30 IST, 23:55 while the US is on winter time; MCX holidays go in Settings (on some NSE holidays MCX trades only its evening session). No AMO for MCX here.






  

    
Option chain

      
        
        
        
        
        
      

    

    

    

    
Live from Kite every 5 seconds in market hours. IV and delta are worked out here from each option's last price (Black-Scholes). OI change is since the desk first read the chain today. Click a price to chart the contract and trade it.

  

  

    

      

    

    

    

      
      
      
      
      
    

    

    

  

  

    
Index futures

      

    

    

    
From Kite. Basis = future − index; annualised it is the market's cost of carry. Click a contract for its candle chart. Day candles are Kite's continuous series, stitched across expired contracts; intraday candles are the contract's own (it lives about three months).

  

  

    

      

    

    

    

  

  

    
Option positions 

    

    
Buying options only: the desk does not sell (write) options here. Paper options left open at expiry are settled at their intrinsic value against the index close.

  

  

    
Futures & options: contracts and expiry dates

      

    

    

    
Indices

    

  

  

    
Stocks

    

  

  
Where this comes from. Kite's instrument list, which the exchange updates every morning. An expired contract drops off and the next one moves up by itself; the desk rebuilds the list each day and whenever the nearest date has passed. Dates are never worked out by rule here, because expiry days change and move for holidays. A lot is the smallest quantity you can trade. The desk shows these contracts but does not place F&O orders.

Exchange holidays

        
One date per line, as YYYY-MM-DD. On these days the desk treats the market as closed: no orders, no algo runs. NSE publishes the list each December (nseindia.com → Resources → Holidays); paste the trading holidays here once a year.

        
        

        
MCX holidays

        
MCX keeps its own list (mcxindia.com → Market Operations → Trading Holidays). One per line: 2026-12-25 closes the whole day; 2026-11-24 evening means only the evening session (17:00 onwards) trades, as on many NSE holidays.

        
      

    

    

      
Two-factor sign-in

      

    

    

      
Passkeys

      
Sign in with your phone's or laptop's fingerprint, face or screen lock instead of the password and code. The device keeps the key; the desk stores only its public half, and a passkey works only on this desk's address, so it cannot be phished. Add one on each device you use. Needs HTTPS.

      

    

    

      
Broker connection (MCP)

      
Standard mcpServers JSON. Zerodha's hosted server mcp.kite.trade gives portfolio and market data but does not place orders. To trade live, run kite-mcp-server with your own Kite Connect API key and point this at it.
